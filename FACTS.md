# Facts stated in the film, and their sources

| Line in the film | Basis | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Even with supercomputers, their forecasts gain only about one day of accuracy every ten years." | Numerical weather prediction skill has improved by roughly one day of lead time per decade (a 6-day forecast today is about as good as a 5-day forecast ten years earlier). Phrased as "about". | Bauer, Thorpe & Brunet, "The quiet revolution of numerical weather prediction", *Nature* 525, 47–55 (2015) |
| "In 1961, the meteorologist Edward Lorenz re-ran a weather simulation, but typed in one number rounded off. 0.506, instead of 0.506127." | Lorenz restarted a run from a printout that showed three decimals (0.506) instead of the six held in memory (0.506127); the new run soon diverged completely. | Lorenz, *The Essence of Chaos* (1993); Gleick, *Chaos: Making a New Science* (1987), ch. 1; Wikipedia, "Edward Norton Lorenz" |
| "Within two simulated months, the forecast described a completely different sky." | In Lorenz's account the difference roughly doubled every four simulated days, so that after about two months the two runs bore no resemblance. | Gleick (1987), ch. 1; Lorenz (1993) |
| "Tomorrow … bends to the smallest touch." | Sensitive dependence on initial conditions (the "butterfly effect"; Lorenz's 1972 talk "Predictability: Does the Flap of a Butterfly's Wings in Brazil Set Off a Tornado in Texas?"). | Lorenz, AAAS meeting, 29 Dec 1972 |
| "In 1948, the sociologist Robert Merton gave this a name: the self-fulfilling prophecy. A false belief that makes itself come true." | Merton defined it as "a false definition of the situation evoking a new behavior which makes the originally false conception come true", illustrated by a run on a sound bank. | Robert K. Merton, "The Self-Fulfilling Prophecy", *The Antioch Review* 8(2), 193–210 (1948) |
| "A bank never keeps all its savers' money in cash. If everyone asks on the same day, it cannot pay." | Fractional-reserve banking: deposits are lent out or invested; a run demands more liquidity than a bank holds. | Diamond & Dybvig, "Bank Runs, Deposit Insurance, and Liquidity", *Journal of Political Economy* 91(3) (1983) |
| "In March 2023, customers of one American bank tried to withdraw $42 billion in a single day. By the next morning, its doors were closed." | Silicon Valley Bank: depositors withdrew about $42 billion on 9 March 2023; the bank was closed by regulators on 10 March. (The film does not name the bank.) | Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, *Review of the Federal Reserve's Supervision and Regulation of Silicon Valley Bank* (April 2023) |
| "The Greeks told of Cassandra, a princess who could see the future, cursed so that no one would ever believe her." | Cassandra, daughter of Priam of Troy, given prophecy by Apollo and cursed never to be believed. | Aeschylus, *Agamemnon*; Apollodorus, *Bibliotheca* III.12.5 |

Fiction: the phone, the courier, Rosa, the dispatcher, the driver, the city of Northgate, Northgate Savings Bank and the Old River Bridge are invented. Nothing in the story claims a real bank failed because of a social-media post.
